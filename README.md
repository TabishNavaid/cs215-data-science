## Final project ideas

### Economic growth and environmental sustainability

I'm working with Kathleen on this project. We want to look at how economic development and environmental sustainability relate across countries. Do countries get greener as they get richer, or does growth usually mean more carbon emissions? We also want to see how renewable energy use has changed over time, and whether some countries have grown their economies while cutting their environmental impact.

### Questions we want to explore

**Do wealthier countries produce more carbon emissions per person?** We'll compare GDP per capita with CO2 emissions per capita, and check whether that link has changed over time.

**Does economic growth lead to more renewable energy use?** We'll look at whether countries whose GDP per capita goes up also get a bigger share of their energy from renewable sources.

**Which countries have grown their economies while cutting emissions?** We'll find countries where GDP went up while total CO2 emissions went down, and look at what they have in common.

**How do these patterns differ by region and income group?** We'll compare high, middle, and low income countries to see if development and sustainability follow the same patterns around the world.

### Data sources

**Our World in Data, CO2 and Greenhouse Gas Emissions dataset.** It has historical emissions, energy use, population, and other environmental data for most countries, which is great for long-term trends. Some values are missing for certain countries or years, and national totals don't count emissions from imported goods.

**World Bank Open Data.** We'll use GDP per capita and maybe other development indicators. The data is fairly standardized across countries, which makes comparisons easier, but coverage varies and GDP alone doesn't capture how developed a country is.

We plan to join the two datasets by country and year, then use visualizations and statistics to understand these relationships without assuming growth is simply good or bad for the environment.
